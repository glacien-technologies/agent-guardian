# Tone rules (non-negotiable)

Every piece of launch copy must satisfy all of the following before it
is posted. These are not suggestions. Security developers and
ML-infrastructure engineers — the target audience — register
marketing-speak in under three seconds and dismiss everything that
follows.

## The rules

1. **Never open with "Excited to announce".** Or any of: "thrilled",
   "delighted", "proud to share", "introducing the future of",
   "revolutionising", "game-changing", "world-class", "best-in-class",
   "next-generation", "unprecedented", "robust", "cutting-edge". If
   the first sentence does not describe the concrete thing the tool
   does, rewrite it.

2. **Lead with the verb.** "Red team your AI agents" beats "A tool
   that helps you red team your AI agents". "Find prompt injection
   before attackers do" beats "Comprehensive solution for prompt-
   injection detection".

3. **Evidence before claims.** Show the scan output, the report, the
   finding, the AIVSS score. Do not say "powerful" — show a screenshot
   of a real finding and let the reader draw the conclusion.

4. **No emojis in technical channels.** HN, Reddit r/netsec,
   r/MachineLearning, r/cybersecurity, GitHub Release: zero. LinkedIn
   and Product Hunt allow a single tasteful emoji per post if it
   carries semantic load. X / Twitter allows up to two per tweet.

5. **No superlatives without a citation.** "The most accurate scanner"
   is forbidden. "AIVSS 7.8 on the LangGraph travel-concierge demo —
   detection in 4 min 12 s" is fine.

6. **Self-deprecation beats self-promotion.** "We built this because
   we kept finding prompt-injection bugs by hand and got tired of it"
   reads honest. "We built the industry's most advanced agent red-
   teaming platform" reads like a press release.

7. **Always link three things.** Repo, live testbench, 60-second demo
   video. Never just one. Readers who land on a single link feel
   trapped.

8. **One question, one ask.** Every post ends with one specific
   request: "feedback on the AIVSS scoring", "tell me what frameworks
   are missing", "what other attack categories should we add". Generic
   "let me know what you think" is a wasted ending.

9. **Respond like a contributor, not a vendor.** When someone reports
   a bug in a comment, the reply is "good catch, filed as #N, fix in
   v1.0.1 by Friday" — not "thanks for the feedback, we'll consider
   it for our roadmap".

10. **No founder LinkedIn voice in Reddit / HN.** "I'm excited to
    share something my team has been working on" reads as out-of-band
    in r/netsec. Open with the technical hook, mention authorship in
    the second paragraph if at all.

## The 30-second test

Read every post aloud. If at any point in the first three sentences
you sound like a product marketing manager, rewrite. If you sound like
an engineer telling another engineer about something they built last
weekend, you are on tone.
